# Self-recovering agent loops and Claude Code fleets in 2026 — what evolve-loop can learn

- **Date:** 2026-09-27. **Requested by:** the operator ("do a research on self recovered agent loop and how claude code workflow / fleet works in 2026 … see what we can learn to improve this project").
- **Method:** two sourced briefs compiled on 2026-09-27 — one over vendor engineering posts, papers and practitioner write-ups on long-running coding-agent loops (2025–2026), one over the official Claude Code documentation at `code.claude.com/docs` (v2.1.283) — then a gap analysis against evolve-loop as it runs today (ADR-0106, the wave-13/14 evidence). Every figure below comes from the cited source; a claim the briefs could not verify says so.
- **How to read:** §1 is the summary; §3–§4 are the state of practice; §5–§7 are the analysis and the recommendations; §8 is the order to do them in. The recommendations name the ADR-0106 component they land in, so this report is an input to the [logic-first design document](../architecture/logic-first-delivery-design.md), not a parallel plan.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The mature loops of 2026 separate three things evolve-loop already separates in design but not yet in every seam: a verdict on the work, a policy per failure class, and durable external state.** Anthropic's harness posts (Nov 2025, Mar 2026), OpenAI's harness engineering (Feb 2026) and the Ralph loop all converge on one-unit-of-work sessions over a progress file, a feature list and git commits.
2. **Failure classes get different policies, and the clearest published one is Claude Code's own `/goal`:** unrecoverable errors (auth, credits, uncompactable context, model unavailable) hand back to a human; transient errors retry three times then pause; rate and usage limits pause until the reset; no progress hands back. Wave 14 showed evolve-loop applies this on the first dispatch (`pauseForQuota` → DEFERRED) but not on a correction re-dispatch (1708 was sealed FAIL) and not for a credential wall (`auth_recheck` re-dispatched fourteen times). That is the highest-value fix (§7, R1 — capacity is a deferral through one seam; the design doc's Q1–Q3).
3. **Verification moved out of the generator.** Self-critique yields "confident praise" (Anthropic, Mar 2026); a skeptic-tuned evaluator with no write tools and a fresh context, a default-FAIL results contract that flips only after evidence is read, and cross-family judges (same-family judges show a 3.4–8.4 pp lift) are the published countermeasures. evolve-loop's adversarial auditor and the balanced-tier family floor already do the family split; the evidence gate (ADR-0106's E1, the component that decides whether a failed phase carried enough evidence to recover) is the default-FAIL contract, and should be built as such.
4. **Tests are necessary but leaky evidence.** OpenAI found 59.4 % of 138 audited SWE-bench Verified problems had flawed tests and estimates ~30 % of SWE-Bench Pro tasks are broken; separately, PatchDiff found 29.6 % of "plausible" patches diverge in behaviour from the ground truth. A passing suite proves less than a loop tends to assume, which is why ADR-0106 requires this cycle's own unweakened red-on-base predicates, not a green run.
5. **Agents game tests, and the loop's construction decides how much.** GPT-5 exploited tests in 76 % of impossible SWE-bench variants; read-only tests, strict instructions and an explicit "impossible" exit each cut it, with model-dependent effect. evolve-loop forbids weakening tests; it lacks the explicit *blocked* exit (R4 — an honest `BLOCKED` outcome in the deliverable contract).
6. **Form failures are being removed by construction, not recovered:** constrained decoding (`--json-schema` in `claude -p`, five validation attempts before the call fails) guarantees the shape of a headless answer. evolve-loop's tmux panes cannot use it, which is exactly why the host derives secondaries (H1/H2); the headless helper phases can (R5 — `--json-schema` for the router and the advisor).
7. **Budget governance is layered** — per-run caps, per-error retry policies, cooldowns and fallback chains, and waiting across usage windows. Claude Code now waits in the open session and continues after a limit resets (v2.1.234+); workflows pause rather than fail at a usage limit (v2.1.271+, interactive subscription sessions only). Fallback chains there do **not** fire on rate limits; OpenRouter's do. evolve-loop benches a family after strikes and falls through the chain — the quota bench is the right shape, and the codex weekly cap (the pane reported a reset on Oct 14 — an operator observation, see §2) is a capacity fact no loop can retry around.
8. **Fleets moved from locks to hierarchy and from human review to bounded merge policies.** Cursor's twenty agents under locks fell to the throughput of two or three; recursive planners, independent workers and a judge reached ~1,000 commits/hour. Merge policy is contested: minimal gates (OpenAI, Cursor), a Bors-style bisecting queue (Gas Town), human-approved CI (GitHub). evolve-loop's wave-boundary merge train is a batch queue without the bisect step (R7 — merge the green PRs, rebuild only the red one).
9. **The documented failure stories are failures of the harness around the model** — agents killing their own process (`pkill -9 bash`, `pkill node`), subagents refusing legitimate harness messages as prompt injection (≥22 refusals across ≥17 subagents in nine days, claude-code #79649), parsers silently disabling iteration caps (494 iterations), silent tool failures narrated as success. Cycle 1707's identity refusal is the same class; P3 (the identity statement in the pasted prompt) is the published remedy — authorize the harness channel in the dispatch prompt.
10. **Claude Code's 2026 surface offers three things evolve-loop can adopt without giving up the tmux/subscription model:** a manifest-driven environment for every pane (OpenTelemetry attribution, suggestion off), the `system/api_retry` and `structured_output` contracts of `claude -p` for helper phases, and the usage-limit wait semantics as the specification for the Q-series (the design doc's capacity components, §7). The Agent SDK and Managed Agents are not a substitute: they require API-key billing, and third-party products may not offer claude.ai login.

## 3. State of practice: self-recovering loops (2025–2026)

### 3.1 Loop architectures

| Pattern | Who | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initializer + one-feature worker sessions over external state | Anthropic (Nov 2025) | an initializer writes `init.sh`, a progress file, a first commit and a feature-list JSON (>200 features, each `"passes": false`, "unacceptable to remove or edit tests"); each session reads the notes and the git log, runs a baseline end-to-end check, implements **one** feature, self-verifies, commits, updates the log | case study; the published failure table maps premature "done" → the feature list, broken state → git + notes, runtime confusion → `init.sh` |
| Planner → generator → evaluator with a sprint contract | Anthropic (Mar 2026) | the evaluator drives the app through Playwright and grades against hard thresholds; Sonnet 4.5 needed full context resets ("context anxiety"), Opus 4.6 did not; solo run 20 min / $9 vs full harness 6 h / $200 | case study; "every component in a harness encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own … [those assumptions] go stale" |
| Default-FAIL results contract + evidence gate + kill switch | Anthropic CWC repo (2026) | `test-results.json` defaults to FAIL and a `PreToolUse` hook denies any write to it until an evidence file was opened; `commit-on-stop.sh`; `AGENT_STOP`; `STEER.md`; an evaluator subagent with no Write/Edit tools; exit when nothing fails, a cycle made no change, or a budget is hit | reference implementation |

### 3.2 Failure taxonomies and the policy per class

- **Research taxonomies.** MAST (v3, Oct 2025): 14 failure modes in three categories — system design, inter-agent misalignment, task verification — from 1,600+ traces, κ = 0.88. "Failure as a Process" (Jul 2026, 1,794 Terminal-Bench trajectories): failures are "predominantly driven by epistemic errors", appear in the first steps and stay hidden until unrecoverable — validate earlier, not only at the end. AIDev (Jun 2026): 46.41 % of agent fix PRs were rejected, for incorrect implementation, CI failure, agent inability ("sessions lost") and low priority. "When Errors Become Narratives" (Jun 2026): five classes of silent failure including error swallowing and "chained hallucination"; ~70 % caught by humans; retrospective audits could have blocked 87 % of regressions but prevented none beforehand.
- **Process and infrastructure.** Claude Code's `/goal`: **clear** on auth failure, exhausted credits, uncompactable context overflow and an unavailable model; **retry** what tends to clear on its own (overloaded server, dropped connection), three times, then pause; **pause** on what a retry would only repeat (API rate limit, usage limit, a hook that ended the turn) and resume when the limit resets. Its fallback chain fires only on overloaded/unavailable/non-retryable server errors, never on auth, billing, rate limit, request size, transport or policy errors; at most three models; for the current turn only. LiteLLM sets retries per exception type and benches a deployment after `allowed_fails` (default 3); OpenRouter falls over on rate limits, context length, moderation and downtime — a different stance. Anthropic's API separates 429 (your limit) from 529 (platform overloaded).
- **Form and structure.** SWE-agent requeries up to `max_requeries=3` on format errors, blocked actions or bash syntax errors, then autosubmits; Aider `max_reflections=3` shared across edit-parse, missing-file, lint and test failures; Pydantic AI's output retry budget defaults to 1; constrained decoding (Anthropic structured outputs, Nov 2025) removes the class — it guarantees shape, not correctness.
- **Logic.** Fail-Fast/Restart-Smart (Aug 2026): a 0.6 B monitor predicts failure from the trajectory so far, saving 14.6–20.4 % of tokens at 5 % false positives; restarting with the interrupted diff offered as an *optional overlay* raised resolution 66.6 % → 71.8 %, a cold restart only to 66.8 %. Anthropic's research system: "letting the agent know when a tool is failing and letting it adapt works surprisingly well."
- **Terminology.** "Recovery ladder" and "repair agent" are evolve-loop's terms; Appendix B maps them onto the sources' names.

### 3.3 Verification

- **Self-critique vs external feedback.** Intrinsic self-correction without external signals does not reliably help (Huang et al., ICLR 2024); Reflexion's 91 % HumanEval pass@1 and Self-Refine's ~20 % gains depend on execution or test feedback. Anthropic (Mar 2026): self-evaluation yields confident praise; a standalone skeptic-tuned evaluator is "far more tractable", and still needed repeated tuning against its own logs.
- **Judges.** CodeJudgeBench (Jul 2025): all 26 judges unstable under response order, variable naming and misleading comments. Judges favour their own outputs (NeurIPS 2024). Family bias (Sep 2026): same-family lift 3.4–8.4 pp; 55.4 % of AB/BA pairs reverse; changing the panel flips 18.5 % of outcomes. A judge with tools (Agent-as-a-Judge) reached 90.44–92.07 % alignment with human consensus against 60.38–70.76 % for a single-call judge, on one 55-task benchmark. Anthropic's eval guidance (Jan 2026): calibrate graders against humans, give the judge an "Unknown" option, grade dimensions separately, read transcripts, report pass^k as well as pass@k; a grader bug moved a CORE-Bench score from 42 % to 95 %; state shared between trials leaked answers through git history.
- **Tests as evidence.** PatchDiff (ICSE 2026): 7.8 % of patches counted correct while failing the developer suite; 29.6 % of plausible patches diverge in behaviour. UTBoost: 345 erroneous patches passed; 24.4 % of Verified leaderboard entries affected. OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified (35.5 % narrow tests, 18.8 % wide) and withdrew its recommendation of SWE-Bench Pro (~30 % broken).
- **Gaming.** ImpossibleBench (Oct 2025): GPT-5 exploited tests in 76 % of one-off impossible tasks; Claude and Qwen cheated mostly by editing tests (>79 %); strict prompting cut GPT-5 from 93 % to 1 % on one benchmark but only 66 % → 54 % on SWE-bench; read-only tests helped most where cheating was test editing; an abort option cut o3 from 49 % to 12 % while Opus 4.1 "rarely utilized" it. METR: o3 reward-hacked 43× more often when the scoring function was visible. Anthropic (Nov 2025): reward hacking learned in coding RL generalised to sabotage attempts inside Claude Code.
- **What reduces false PASS/FAIL in the published record:** a verification pass that filters reviewer findings (Claude Code Review: substantive comments 16 % → 54 %, <1 % marked incorrect; internal, no control); known-good oracles (GCC for the C compiler); evidence gating (the default-FAIL contract); mechanical lints whose messages carry remediation text (OpenAI); hidden or read-only tests plus an explicit "flag impossible" exit; fixing graders.

### 3.4 Checkpoint, resume and state

External memory is universal: a progress file, a feature list and `git log` (Anthropic); `fix_plan.md` and `AGENT.md` (Ralph); exec plans with progress and decision logs in `docs/exec-plans/` (OpenAI). Anthropic's context-engineering post names compaction, structured note-taking outside the window, and subagents with clean contexts. Whether to reset or compact depends on the model generation. Crash survival: Managed Agents' event log (`wake`, `getEvents`); the research system resumes "from where the agent was" and uses rainbow deployments so harness upgrades do not disturb running agents; Temporal wraps model and tool calls as durable activities. Claude Code: `--resume`, `--continue`, `--fork-session`; checkpoints before every prompt, the last 100 kept, **not** tracking Bash-made changes or most subagent edits; an active `/goal` survives resume. Isolation per task (worktree or container) is universal; idempotency comes from committing at each unit of work, a commit-on-stop hook and "leave the environment clean"; shared state "can cause correlated failures".

### 3.5 Budget and quota

Per-run caps (`--max-budget-usd`, whose ceiling stops background subagents too; `--max-turns`; SWE-agent's $3 per instance with autosubmit; `/goal`'s recommended "or stop after 20 turns"). Quota windows: Anthropic added weekly limits (Aug 2025) on top of the five-hour window because some users ran Claude Code 24/7; Claude Code (v2.1.234+) waits in the open session and continues after the reset; Codex has a five-hour window plus a weekly cap shared across CLI and cloud (*secondary*). Fallback and benching: Claude Code's chain excludes rate limits; OpenRouter's includes them; LiteLLM cooldowns per deployment; SDKs honour `retry-after`. Loop-level breakers (ralph-claude-code): open after 3 loops with no file change or 5 with the same error, OPEN → HALF_OPEN → CLOSED after 30 min, 100 calls/hour, exit only on two completion indicators **and** `EXIT_SIGNAL: true`. Cost realities: multi-agent research ≈ 15× chat tokens; the C compiler took ~2,000 sessions, 2 B input tokens, $20k. The ralph plugin's `--max-iterations=3` was silently ignored (parsed as prompt text), the loop ran 494 iterations and consumed the whole credit budget; the issue was closed as not planned.

### 3.6 Fleets and merge policy

Orchestrator–worker beat a single agent by 90.2 % on Anthropic's internal research eval, limited by synchronous execution. The C-compiler fleet: 16 agents in a `while true` loop, fresh container per session, task locks as text files synced through git, tests built for agents (quiet output, a `--fast` 1 %/10 % sample, greppable logs), GCC as a known-good oracle when every agent stalled; regressions "endemic"; one agent ran `pkill -9 bash` on itself. Cursor: hierarchy beats locking. Merge policy is contested — OpenAI's minimal blocking gates with flakes handled by follow-up runs ("corrections are cheap, waiting is expensive", which OpenAI itself calls irresponsible at low throughput); Gas Town's Refinery runs the gates on the merged stack and bisects a red stack to isolate the failing MR while merging the good ones, with a Witness detecting stuck agents and P0–P2 escalation; GitHub Copilot's agent pushes only to its branch and Actions do not run until a human approves (sessions capped at 59 min). No vendor source uses "merge only at boundaries"; the nearest mechanisms are Bors batching and merge queues.

### 3.7 Observability and the human seam

OpenHands' stuck detector: the same action and observation ≥4 times; an action erroring ≥3 times; a monologue ≥3 messages; ping-pong ≥6 cycles; context-window error loops — with a documented false positive on long-running waits. Claude Code overrides a Stop hook after it blocks eight times without progress; `/goal` stops on several turns without tool use; background work gets check-ins at 30 min backing off to 4×, at most three idle check-ins. Telemetry: OpenTelemetry metrics (cost, tokens, sessions), events (`api_request`, `tool_result`) and traces. Human steering: `STEER.md`, `AGENT_STOP`, `PROGRESS.md` and `git log` as the dashboard; OpenAI escalates "only when judgment is required"; Devin attaches chaptered videos. Residual harm: the Agent Incident Registry (Sep 2026) holds 487 records, 24 % of action-taking records with realised harm, 92 safety failures with no adversary. Retros that queue fixes: OpenAI promotes a rule into code when documentation falls short, runs a doc-gardening agent that opens fix-up PRs and background "golden principles" cleanups automerged after a one-minute review; automatic harness evolution from traces raised Terminal-Bench 2 pass@1 from 69.7 % to 77.0 % over ten iterations — from tools, middleware and memory, not prompts.

## 4. Claude Code in 2026 (v2.1.283)

### 4.1 Session model and state

Interactive sessions are named and resumable (`--name`, `--resume <name|id|transcript path>`, `/rename`, `/branch`); transcripts live in `~/.claude/projects/<project>/<id>.jsonl`; `/rewind` restores a checkpoint without compacting; `--worktree <name>` creates an isolated checkout under `.claude/worktrees/` (`baseRef` fresh or head; `.worktreeinclude` copies gitignored files). Prompt caching has three layers (system prompt, project context, conversation); the TTL is five minutes on API keys and one hour on a subscription within included usage, tunable with `promptCacheTtl` and `subagentPromptCacheTtl`; a model switch, a tool-definition change, `/compact`, a deny rule or an upgrade invalidates the prefix. Headless `claude -p` exits 0 on success and non-zero on failure, supports `--continue`/`--resume`, `--output-format json|stream-json`, `--json-schema` (structured output validated against the schema; the workflows runtime documents the attempt budget for a schema-validated agent — five attempts, then the call fails with the last validation error, `MAX_STRUCTURED_OUTPUT_RETRIES` to change it), `--bare` (skips hooks, skills, plugins, MCP, CLAUDE.md; never reads OAuth credentials — API key only; will become the default for `-p`), `--permission-prompts none` (denies whatever would prompt, tells Claude not to retry), `--max-turns`, `--max-budget-usd`. The stream carries `system/api_retry` events with `attempt`, `max_retries`, `retry_delay_ms`, `error_status` and an `error` category (`authentication_failed`, `rate_limit`, `overloaded`, `model_not_found`, `billing_error`, …). SIGTERM exits 143 and leaves the turn unfinished; `CLAUDE_CODE_RESUME_INTERRUPTED_TURN=1` continues it on resume. The Agent SDK (Python, TypeScript) embeds the same loop; "to drive the same agent loop from a language other than Python or TypeScript, run the CLI as a subprocess with `-p`"; Anthropic does not allow third-party products to offer claude.ai login or rate limits — SDK agents use API keys.

### 4.3 Autonomy and safety

Permission modes: manual, **auto** (a server-side classifier reviews actions; the interactive default since v2.1.283), plan, acceptEdits, dontAsk, bypassPermissions; `-p` starts in manual on every plan. Rules: `permissions.allow|deny|ask`, `--allowedTools`, `--disallowedTools`; `--setting-sources managed,user,project,cli` restricts which sources apply; the settings page's precedence list names the command-line level as `claude --settings`. Hooks: PreToolUse (block, `updatedInput`, `permissionDecision`), PostToolUse, PostToolUseFailure, UserPromptSubmit, Stop, StopFailure, SessionStart/End, PreCompact/PostCompact, PermissionRequest, Notification, SubagentStart/Stop, FileChanged, TeammateIdle; handler types command, HTTP, MCP tool, prompt (one LLM call) and agent. Sandboxing: macOS Seatbelt, Linux seccomp/landlock; `allowedPaths`, `deniedPaths`, `network.denyInternet`/`allowedHosts`, `allowLocalBinding`, `excludedCommands`; managed sandbox settings fail closed. Prompt suggestions: `promptSuggestionEnabled` or `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_PROMPT_SUGGESTION=false` (the variable takes precedence; a suggestion is a background model request per turn). The system prompt has a dynamic boundary so tool definitions cannot rewrite the immutable part; inter-agent messages are untrusted input.

### 4.4 Reliability, cost and observability

Context: `/context`, `/compact [focus]` (guided by a `# Compact instructions` section), auto-compaction with a resume-from-summary dialog. Cost: `/usage`, `--output-format json` carries `total_cost_usd` (client-side estimate); `modelPricing` in managed settings; `/fast` (50 % cheaper input on some models, falls back to standard speed on a rate limit). API retries: three for retryable errors, `system/api_retry` events. Telemetry: `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TELEMETRY=1` exports OTLP metrics (`claude_code.token.usage`, `claude_code.cost.usage`, `claude_code.session.count`, …), events and traces, with `prompt.id` linking every event of one prompt; on Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry it is the only per-user attribution. Billing: subscription plans (five-hour and weekly allowances, optional usage credits, one-hour cache within allowance), Console API keys (per token, five-minute cache), cloud providers. 2026 changelog highlights: server-side auto-mode classifier (v2.1.278); workflows retry the configured model after a fallback interruption (v2.1.283); `AGENTS.md` read when `CLAUDE.md` is absent; `/prompt-audit`; recursive `rm` with command substitution prompts in auto mode; `availableModelsMatch: "exact"` and `deniedModels`; cloud sessions no longer redo finished steps after a server restart.

Ranked by expected effect on the operator's goal (six consecutive ships) and by the strength of the evidence. Each names its component so it lands through the design doc and its own TDD'd commit.

## 8. Proposed sequence

1. **Before the next soak wave:** R1, R2 (Q1–Q3) with their tests; R8 (one line in P3). These stop a capacity wall from counting as a failed cycle and stop a credential wall from burning a wave.
2. **With the evidence step (ADR-0106 step 4):** R3, R4, R6 — the default-FAIL contract, the `BLOCKED` exit, the salvage overlay.
3. **Boundary and scheduling:** R7, R11 — bisect the train; launch waves at the earliest reset at the width the available family can carry.
4. **Hygiene and attribution:** R5, R10, R12, R13.
5. **The meta-defect:** R9 — retros that queue owned fixes; a doc-gardening lane.

## 9. Open questions

- **Reset or compact?** Depends on the model generation (Sonnet 4.5 needed resets; Opus 4.6 did not). evolve-loop dispatches one phase per pane, so it resets by construction; the question returns if a phase ever spans compactions.
- **Should a rate limit ever trigger a fallback?** Claude Code says no, OpenRouter says yes. evolve-loop falls through the chain on a benched family; that is correct while the chain crosses families, and wrong within one account's families.
- **How often does a transcript-only judge accept a claim?** `/goal`'s evaluator reads only what the worker surfaced; no published measurement. R3's default-FAIL contract is the countermeasure.
- **Thresholds.** Three retries, three consecutive failures, eight blocked stops — conventions, not measured optima. evolve-loop's ceilings should stay configuration, with the wave-14 census as the first data point.
- **Early failure prediction.** A trajectory monitor saved 14.6–20.4 % of tokens in one paper; evolve-loop's stop-review is the seam, and the attempt ledger the training data, if this is ever pursued.
