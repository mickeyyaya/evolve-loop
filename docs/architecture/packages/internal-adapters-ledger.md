This page keeps the package-level detail those do not.

## Purpose

`internal/adapters/ledger` is the file-backed `core.Ledger`: an append-only JSONL provenance record, hash-chained over each line's raw bytes. It also owns the operator tools that keep the chain verifiable as it grows and collects historical damage: segment sealing, epoch anchors (a sidecar file and in-band seals), bulk rebaseline, kernel-recomputed composition verdicts with their durable evidence and its restore from git, and the Signal Center append observer.

## Design

- **Files under the evolve dir.** `ledger.jsonl` (one line per entry), `ledger.tip` (`<seq>:<sha256 of the last line>`), `ledger.lock` (the append flock), `ledger.lock.seal` (the seal flock), `ledger-anchor.json` (the sidecar anchor), `ledger-segments/seg-NNNN.jsonl.gz` (sealed history) and `ledger-artifacts/` (the content-addressed evidence store, owned by [`internal/ledgerartifacts`](internal-ledgerartifacts.md); gc hard-protects it).
- **The chain.** Each entry's `prev_hash` is the SHA-256 of the previous line's bytes as written, and `entry_seq` counts up from 0. A genesis entry carries `ZeroSeed` (64 ASCII zeros). The package began as a port of `scripts/observability/verify-ledger-chain.sh`, and that script's conventions carried over: the zero seed, and no tip for a ledger with no chained line.
- **One write path.** `appendChained` (tip-based, the hot path) and `appendChainedFromTail` (chains from the physical last line, the repair path) share `appendLineAndReplaceTip`. `Append`, `AppendLifecycle`, `WriteCompositionVerdict` and the evidence restore use the first, `Rebaseline` the second. The repair path reads the whole file per call, which is acceptable for an operator command and wrong for every phase boundary. Both take `mu` and the `ledger.lock` flock through one helper, `underChainLock`.
- **Verification reads one snapshot.** `Verify` and `VerifyDeep` read the chain and `ledger.tip` together under `readSnapshot` (the read lock of `mu`, now an `RWMutex`, and a shared `flock.LockShared` on `ledger.lock`), then walk outside it. Appenders take the exclusive lock through `underChainLock`, so a reader waits for an append and an append for the readers, while readers never wait for each other; the shared lock opens `ledger.lock` read-only, so a reader verifies a ledger in a directory it cannot write. An absent `ledger.lock` means no writer ever appended there, so the read runs unlocked and creates nothing; any other failure to open or take the lock is an error, never an unlocked read (`TestVerify_FailsLoudlyWhenTheChainLockCannotBeOpened`). An absent live ledger answers `Verify` without reading at all. Open: live-only `Verify` cannot verify a sealed ledger, and ship's carry check uses it, so sealing the plane would disable every carry (inbox `seal-breaks-live-only-verify-and-carry`, a hard prerequisite of the plan's C12 and C13). Before 2026-10-06 they read the file, walked it (seconds on the 49 MB plane ledger) and only then read the tip, so a peer's append in between was reported as a tip mismatch, which also declined a carry. The lock is held only for the two reads, and it is skipped when no ledger exists, so verifying never creates a lock file in a directory that has no ledger. `VerifyDeep` also gunzips the segments under the lock; on a large sealed ledger that blocks appenders for the read (the plan's C12 replaces it with an incremental verify).
- **One chain walk.** `walkChain` (the epoch skip in `epochStart`, one line's chain rules in `chainCursor.acceptChained`) serves both `Verify` (the live file) and `VerifyDeep` (the gunzipped segments followed by the live tail), so the two cannot disagree on what "intact" means. `VerifyDeep` and `Rebaseline`'s dry run share `readChain` and `verifyChain`, so the refusal decision cannot drift from the verdict.
- **Soft-start boundary.** A line with no `prev_hash` key (written before v8.37 added chain hashing) is hashed but not validated. The chained region starts with a seq-0 zero-seeded entry or with one chained from the last unchained line; both occur in real histories.
- **Accepted seams.** Real history carries two benign shapes, and the walk accepts exactly these:
  - *Re-genesis*: `Append` against a missing tip re-seeds the chain (seq 0, zero prev). The seam stays visible, every later line still hashes over its bytes, and the tip and segment anchors bind the end state.
  - *Fork sibling*: concurrent appends that raced the tip before the flock existed wrote several entries off one parent. A line is accepted as a sibling when its prev equals the previous line's prev (never the zero seed), and the chain resumes from the last sibling. The racy `entry_seq` is unreliable; the hash linkage is what to trust.
- **Epoch anchors.** Damage that is real but adjudicated is preserved, never deleted, and stops being validated past an anchor. `effectiveAnchorSHA` resolves two sources and the later one wins:
  - the sidecar `ledger-anchor.json`, written by `evolve ledger anchor <seq> [--line-sha]` and bound to the line's current SHA, so any later change to that line invalidates the anchor;
  - an in-band operator seal, a chain entry with `Role: "operator"` and the `reset-seal-` marker in `Kind` or `CycleLabel`. It exists because a recovery that resumes the chain should leave its sign-off inside the auditable record, not in a sidecar that can be lost, copied or drift from it.
- **Verified scope.** `VerifyScope` and `VerifyDeepScope` report which history a successful walk covered: the anchor line's SHA and its own `entry_seq`, or the zero value for "every line from genesis". The CLI prints it next to `OK`.
- **Rebaseline.** `evolve ledger anchor` binds one line and the last anchor wins, so it cannot repair a prefix with many dense breaks. `Rebaseline` appends one `RebaselineKind` in-band seal at the physical tail, which moves the anchor past every break behind it. Before it writes, it dry-runs `VerifyDeep`'s verdict (`verifyChain`: the walk, segment binding and tip) over the chain it already read plus the prospective seal line, and refuses on a would-fail result. After the append it runs `VerifyDeep` again, which now fails only when a writer raced in between.
- **Segment sealing.** `Seal` moves all but the newest `keepTail` lines, byte for byte, into the next gzip segment and appends a `segment_seal` entry: `ArtifactPath` is the segment path relative to the evolve dir (portable across checkouts) and `ArtifactSHA256` is the SHA of the uncompressed bytes. Concatenating the gunzipped segments with the live tail reproduces the pre-seal file exactly. The format is gzip rather than the planned zstd because the standard library has no zstd and the module takes no production dependency for it; the ratio is ample for JSONL.
- **Crash windows.** Each is detected by `VerifyDeep` as `ErrSealResidue` and completed by re-running `Seal`:
  - *A*: the segment was written but the live file was not truncated (the segment's first line is still live). `Seal` truncates only after the whole segment matches the live prefix; a mismatch asks for manual inspection.
  - *B*: the file was truncated but the anchor entry never landed. `Seal` appends the missing anchor.
- **Composition verdicts.** A `composition-verdict` line records an audit verdict carried forward across a conflict-free trivial rebase (RUNG 0, method `trivial-rebase`) or a scoped merge review (RUNG 2, method `scoped-review`). Review verdicts follow the change's `git patch-id --stable`; gates follow the tree. The line is kernel-recomputable: `walkChain` re-derives the patch-id of both diffs and compares it with the recorded one, at zero LLM cost. `WriteCompositionVerdict` produces the line and `ship.tryTrivialRebaseCarryForward` reads it; `compositionRecord` is the union of ship's `compositionEntry` and this package's `compositionFields`. The `evolve ledger verify` exit-code contract for these lines is pinned in `cmd/evolve/cmd_ledger_composition_test.go`. Since ADR-0105 B3 a second method, `identical-rebase`, records a byte-identical rebase carrying the audited verdict; its line also names `audited_tree_sha`, and `LatestCompositionVerdict(ledgerPath, method, laneAuditRef)` is the reader ship uses (the newest record for an audit wins; an absent ledger is no carry). Pinned by `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_IdenticalRebaseRecordsTheAuditedTreeAndReadsBack`.
- **Composition evidence is content-addressed.** `WriteCompositionVerdict` refuses a ledger path that is a symbolic link (a worktree's `.evolve/ledger.jsonl` links to the project ledger, but the writer would take its tip, lock and store from the link's own directory and fork the chain), validates its input (`validateCompositionInput`), then puts both diffs into the store beside the ledger it writes (`ledgerartifacts.Open(dir(ledgerPath))`) and records `audited_diff_sha256` and `composed_diff_sha256`. The two `*_diff_path` fields name the store objects, so an older binary's verify, which reads only the path, still finds them. The caller has no say in where the diffs go: until 2026-10-06 it passed an `ArtifactDir`, the identity carry passed its cycle worktree, and the diffs died with the worktree while their line lived on in the project ledger (ADR-0105 F5). `verifyCompositionLine` resolves each diff in order: the recorded sha in the store; for a line written before the store, a later chained `composition-evidence` record that names the line by its sha256 (`indexCompositionEvidence`, one byte-prefiltered pass over the lines before the walk); then the recorded path. The resolved bytes must still re-derive the recorded patch-id, so an evidence record says where to look, never what to trust.
- **Evidence restore.** `RestoreCompositionEvidence` (`evolve ledger evidence restore`) and `PreviewCompositionEvidence` (`… --dry-run`) make every composition line's evidence durable without rewriting any line. Both run one walk; what differs is a Strategy, `evidenceSink`: `ledgerSink` puts each diff into the store and appends the chained record, `previewSink` only computes the digest and records nothing. The force is the flag-argument rule: a dry run must take exactly the decision path the real run takes, and differ only in what it persists. The git reads go through one adapter, `gitDelta` (`gitexec` under `treedelta.Delta`), the package's single home for that process boundary, as `PatchID` already is for `git patch-id`. The sources a diff may come from (its recorded path, then its git objects) are an ordered Chain of Responsibility, `evidenceSource`: the first whose bytes prove out wins, and each refusal is kept for the reason. For each diff the store cannot already produce, it tries the recorded path, then, for an `identical-rebase` line, the commits and trees the line names (`treedelta.Delta`, the flags the carry used). It accepts the first source whose bytes re-derive the recorded patch-id (and hash to the recorded sha, when the line has one), stores them, and appends one chained `composition-evidence` record (role `operator`) per line that had no sha fields, only when both diffs proved out (a record is permanent; see the incident's known limits). The two diffs are named slots (`evidenceSlots.audited`, `.composed`), each regenerated from the objects its own fields name. Like Verify, it starts after the epoch anchor (`epochStart`), so it never refuses a line Verify does not check. A trivial-rebase or scoped-review line names no git tree (its `tree_state_sha` is a content hash), so it can only be kept from its path. Each line reports `durable`, `stored`, `rebuilt` or `unrestorable` with the reason; a dry run stores and appends nothing.
- **Append observer.** `WithSignals` installs `onAppend` at construction, so `Append` is the single point every observed entry passes. A Decorator embedding `*FileLedger` was rejected: Go embedding has no virtual dispatch, so the promoted `AppendLifecycle` and `Seal` would have written unobserved. The writers that are still unobserved (`Rebaseline`, `WriteCompositionVerdict`, `RestoreCompositionEvidence`, and `PreviewCompositionEvidence`, which records nothing but which the name-keyed scan cannot tell apart) are inventoried with reasons in `TestFileLedger_EveryLineWriterReachesTheAppendChokepointOrIsInventoried`.
- **`LifecycleRecord`** lets `inboxmover` write chained lifecycle entries without importing `core`: `core`'s tests exercise the real `inboxmover`, so that import would form a test-package cycle.
- **Test seams.** `ledgerHooks` and the `anchorCreateTemp`/`anchorWrite`/`anchorClose` variables exist only so tests can reach I/O error branches.

## Invariants

- **Every ledger line goes through the chained write path.** `Append` chains from the tip while `walkChain` checks physical predecessors, so one raw write desynchronizes them and breaks the next entry. Pinned by `TestFileLedger_EveryLineWriterReachesTheAppendChokepointOrIsInventoried` and `TestRebaseline_ForeignUnchainedTailLine_SealsAndVerifies`; a one-line why sits on `appendChained`.
- **Appends are serialized across goroutines and processes.** `mu` covers goroutines and the blocking flock on `ledger.lock` covers processes, around the whole tip-read→append→tip-write section. Pinned by `TestAppend_TwoProcessStress` (the chain verifies, every entry lands, `entry_seq` is unique and gapless).
- **The tip is replaced atomically** (temp file plus rename), so a concurrent reader never sees a truncated tip. Pinned by `TestAppend_TwoProcessStress`; a one-line why is in code.
- **A repair seal chains from the physical last line, and the tip then moves to it.** Foreign raw lines can sit past the tip, and `sealChainsFromPrev` checks the physical predecessor. Pinned by `TestRebaseline_ForeignUnchainedTailLine_SealsAndVerifies`, which also appends after the seal to prove the tip moved.
- **Seam acceptance is exact.** Accepted: re-genesis (seq 0 with zero prev) and fork siblings, in runs of any length. Broken: zero prev with a nonzero seq, a prev matching nothing, and a duplicate prev that is not adjacent. Sibling runs are unbounded because an attacker gains nothing from them without control of `ledger.tip`. Pinned by `TestWalkChain_ReGenesisSeamAccepted`, `TestWalkChain_ZeroPrevWithNonzeroSeqIsBroken`, `TestWalkChain_ForkSiblingAccepted`, `TestWalkChain_ThreeForkSiblingsAccepted`, `TestWalkChain_PrevMatchingNothingIsBroken` and `TestWalkChain_DuplicatePrevWithoutSiblingSignatureIsBroken`.
- **Soft boundary.** Unchained lines are not validated, the first chained line must be a genesis or chain from the last unchained line, and a ledger with no chained line needs no tip. Pinned by `TestVerify_SoftBoundary_Mixed`, `TestVerify_SoftBoundary_FirstV837_WrongPrev` and `TestVerify_SoftBoundary_AllPreV837`.
- **An anchor only ever trusts the prefix behind it.** Lines after it are strict, and reaching it turns on the tip check. An anchor SHA that no line carries is a chain break, never a silent fallback to "no anchor", while an unreadable or corrupt sidecar degrades to full-strict verification. Neither case can relax the walk. Pinned by `TestWalkChain_Anchor_SkipsPreEpochDamage`, `TestWalkChain_Anchor_PostAnchorBreakStillCaught`, `TestWalkChain_AnchorNotFound_Errors`, `TestWalkChain_AnchorLineMutated_NotFound`, `TestVerify_HonorsAnchorFile` and `TestLoadAnchorSHA_CorruptJSON`.
- **In-band seal trust.** A seal moves the anchor only if its Role is exactly `operator` and it chains from its own physical predecessor. Role is self-declared, so the unattended autoseal path writes `operator-autoseal`; otherwise any way of killing an owner process could mint a trust anchor. Without the self-validity check, one forged operator line would silence the entire prefix. A line with no `prev_hash` carries no self-proof and never anchors. Pinned by `TestEffectiveAnchorSHA_AutosealRole_DoesNotMoveAnchor`, `TestEffectiveAnchorSHA_OperatorRole_MovesAnchor`, and in `go/acs/cycle1191` by `TestC1191_001_seal_anchor_resolves_production_seal_shape`, `TestC1191_002_self_invalid_seal_must_not_anchor` and `TestC1191_003_break_after_last_seal_still_broken`.
- **The later anchor wins, and the scope names the line actually resumed from.** Seals before the sidecar anchor sit in the untrusted prefix and are ignored. The scope's seq comes from the resolved line, because the sidecar's `anchor_seq` goes stale once an in-band seal moves past it. Pinned by `TestVerifyScope_SeqIsReadFromTheAnchorLineNotTheSidecar` and `TestVerifyScope_SidecarOnlyAnchorReportsThatLine`.
- **A failed verification claims no scope, and both paths report the same scope.** A caller must never print a sealed-prefix claim for a rejected chain. Pinned by `TestVerifyScope_BrokenChainClaimsNoScope` and `TestVerifyDeepScope_ReportsTheSameScopeAsVerifyScope`.
- **Anchoring binds exactly one line.** A seq must be carried by one distinct line; distinctness is by SHA, so byte-identical duplicates count once. An ambiguous seq returns `ErrAmbiguousAnchorSeq`, which the CLI keys its `--line-sha` hint on. A named SHA must itself carry the seq, so the flag cannot bind an arbitrary line. The search covers sealed segments, and no anchor file is left on any error. Pinned by `TestAnchor_RejectsAmbiguousSeq`, `TestAnchor_RejectsAmbiguousSeq_ByteIdenticalLinesAreNotAmbiguous`, `TestAnchor_LineSHABindsNamedSibling`, `TestAnchor_LineSHANegatives`, `TestAnchor_FindsSealedSegmentLine`, `TestAnchor_SeqNotFound_Errors` and the tests in `anchor_write_error_test.go`.
- **`Anchor` takes neither `mu` nor a flock.** It reads the whole chain and writes the sidecar through a unique temp file and an atomic rename. Racing a `Seal`, it may miss a line that is mid-relocation, but it never binds a line the seal removed. Pinned by `TestAnchor_ConcurrentWithSeal_NoCorruptAnchor`.
- **Rebaseline is gated and append-only.** It needs a non-blank operator note (an unattributable trust decision is worse than a red chain) and refuses an empty chain (a sealed genesis would fabricate an audit record). It never rewrites or truncates. A seal that would not deep-verify (a stale sidecar anchor, an unanchored segment, a malformed tip) is refused with every ledger file byte-identical and the cause wrapped. Pinned by `TestRebaseline_SealsDamagedPrefix`, `TestRebaseline_PreservesDamagedPrefixBytes`, `TestRebaseline_RefusesUngatedAndEmptyChain` and `TestRebaseline_RefusesWriteWhenForwardWalkWouldFail`.
- **Sealing never rewrites history.** Lines move as their original bytes and are never re-marshaled. Pinned by `TestSeal_ChainStaysVerifiableEndToEnd` and `TestRewriteLive`.
- **Seal lock order.** `Seal` takes `ledger.lock.seal`, then `ledger.lock`; `Append` takes only `ledger.lock`, so there is no inversion. The seal lock stops a second `Seal`'s resume path from anchoring a segment the first has not anchored yet (a one-line why in code; no test drives two Seals). `Seal` releases `ledger.lock` between truncation and the anchor `Append`, and a foreign process that appends in that window must lose nothing. Pinned by `TestSeal_ConcurrentWithCrossProcessAppend_ChainStaysVerifiable`. Its mutation check: drop the inner `flock.Lock(l.lockPath)` from `Seal` and `rewriteLive`'s rename drops a foreign append, so the child's count falls below `stressN`.
- **Seal residue is detected on every segment, and the resume paths complete it.** Pinned by `TestSeal_ResumeAfterCrashBeforeTruncate` and `TestSeal_ResumeAfterCrashBeforeAnchor`.
- **Every segment is bound by a `segment_seal` entry** whose SHA equals the segment's uncompressed bytes. Pinned by `TestVerifyDeep_TamperedSegmentFails` and `TestSeal_AnchorKindIsSealKind`.
- **Segment names use a fixed-width counter**, so lexical order is chronological (a one-line why in code). `keepTail` is at least 1.
- **A committed diff that cannot be produced is a chain break (D6).** The store is resolved first and the path last, and every resolved diff must re-derive the recorded patch-id. A line whose stored object is gone breaks, a legacy line resolves through a later evidence record, and an evidence record pointing at other bytes still breaks. Pinned by `TestVerify_ResolvesACompositionLineFromTheStoreBeforeItsPath`, `TestVerify_AStoredDiffThatIsGoneIsStillAChainBreak`, `TestVerify_ResolvesALegacyCompositionLineThroughALaterChainedEvidenceRecord`, `TestVerify_AnEvidenceRecordStillHasToReDeriveThePatchID`, and across packages by `core` `TestCarryRecord_VerifiesAfterItsCycleWorktreeIsRemoved`.
- **Restore never rewrites a line and never trusts a regenerated diff on its own word.** Only bytes that re-derive the recorded patch-id are stored; a refused line stores and appends nothing; a second run finds everything durable and appends nothing. A line that names its own diffs by sha, or that already has an evidence record, gets no second record when its objects are rebuilt; a line with one unprovable diff gets none; a rebuilt diff must hash to the sha the line records; restore skips pre-anchor lines and refuses a ledger whose anchor line is gone; and Verify breaks on a stored diff whose bytes no longer hash to its name, without falling back to the path (`…_AppendsNoRecordWhenOneDiffCannotBeProven`, `…_RefusesARebuiltDiffThatDoesNotHashToTheRecordedDigest`, `…_SkipsLinesBeforeTheEpochAnchorAsVerifyDoes`, `…_RefusesALedgerWhoseAnchorLineIsGone`, `TestVerify_AStoredDiffWhoseBytesNoLongerHashToItsNameIsAChainBreak`, and for sealed history `TestVerifyDeep_ResolvesASealedLegacyLineThroughItsSealedEvidenceRecord`). Pinned by `TestRestoreCompositionEvidence_RebuildsALegacyCarryFromTheGitObjectsItsLineNames`, `…_DryRunWritesNothing`, `…_RefusesADiffThatDoesNotReDeriveTheRecordedPatchID`, `…_StoresALegacyDiffItsWorktreeStillHolds`, `…_NamesALineItCannotRegenerate`, `…_RebuildsALostStoredDiffWithoutAnEvidenceRecord` and `…_RebuildsAnAliasedLinesLostDiffsWithoutASecondRecord`.
- **A composition line is written only through the ledger it names.** Pinned by `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_RefusesALedgerPathThatIsALinkToAnotherLedger`.
- **Readers share the lock and need no write access.** Pinned by `TestVerify_HoldsTheChainLockSharedSoReadersDoNotSerialize` (a second reader's non-blocking shared flock succeeds while Verify reads) and `TestVerify_ReadsALedgerInADirectoryItCannotWrite` (skipped as root); an absent ledger is not read (`TestVerify_AnAbsentLedgerIsNotRead`).
- **Verification sees one consistent chain and tip, and leaves nothing behind where no ledger exists.** Pinned by `TestVerify_APeerAppendBetweenTheChainAndTipReadsIsNotABreak` (Verify and VerifyDeep), whose peer takes a turn after the chain read and before the tip read and appends through `flock.TryLock` only when the chain is unlocked, so the interleaving is deterministic and a tip read moved outside the lock fails it; and by `TestVerify_ADirectoryWithNoLedgerVerifiesWithoutCreatingALock`.
- **Composition verdicts fail closed at write time.** The path must be a `ledger.jsonl`, both diffs must be non-blank (a whitespace-only diff counts as blank), every `ciparity.RequiredComposedGates` gate must pass, and the claimed patch-id must recompute from both diffs. Otherwise nothing is appended, so no line can land that verify would flag as tampered; a diff the store cannot keep also appends nothing (`TestWriteCompositionVerdict_AppendsNothingWhenTheDiffsCannotBeStored`), and two carries of one cycle never overwrite each other's evidence (`TestWriteCompositionVerdict_TwoCarriesOfOneCycleBothStayVerifiable`). At verify time a drifted diff, a forged or missing `patch_id`, or a deleted artifact breaks the chain with `core.ErrLedgerChainBroken`. Pinned by `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_RoundTrip`, `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_RejectsPatchIDMismatch`, `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_EmptyDiff`, `TestWriteCompositionVerdict_MethodDefaultsTrivialRebase`, `TestVerify_CompositionVerdictKind_KernelRecompute`, `TestVerify_CompositionVerdict_MissingArtifactBreaksChain` and `TestPatchID_StableContentIdentity`.
- **Every entry through `Append` is one `ledger.appended` signal.** Success is INFO naming `fields.entry_seq`; failure is WARN `LEDGER_APPEND_FAILED` and the error is still returned. The last `WithSignals` option wins, and a nil Center leaves the ledger unobserved. Pinned by the tests in `signals_test.go`.

## Findings

- **Line 1740** (cycle-107 era, 2026-05-26): a predecessor's bytes were rewritten after the fact, so entry 1740 chains from a hash that matches nothing. `evolve ledger verify` correctly stayed red, and ADR-0048 chose the non-destructive epoch anchor over a destructive rebaseline. The same line held a string `cycle`, which broke the dispatcher's reader; `core.LedgerEntry` now routes such values to `CycleLabel`, and `TestIter_RealLedger_NoStringCycleError` iterates the real ledger when one is reachable.
- **L3.3 acceptance** (segment sealing): the strict walk turned out red on the real file for two benign reasons. One was a re-genesis at line 15 (2026-05-07, the day chain hashing landed). The other was fork siblings at lines 263/264 (equal seqs) and 273 (a +1 seq). Both became tightly signed carve-outs. The planned "deep verify green on a real-ledger copy" was replaced by verdict preservation (`TestSeal_RealLedgerCopy`), because blessing the line-1740 class would gut the verifier.
- **CA.1** (concurrency-factory plan): two `evolve` processes, such as a fleet supervisor and a cycle or the cycle 278/279 two-session launch race, interleaved tip reads and appends and broke the chain. The flock on `ledger.lock` fixed it. The RED stress run also caught a torn tip read (`tip malformed: ""`), which led to the atomic tip replace. The flock blocks with no timeout; [concurrency-isolation research](../concurrency-isolation-research-2026-06-14.md) tracks stale-lock liveness as open.
- **cycle-1191**: `isOperatorSeal` matched `Kind` only, while `core.SealCycle` writes the marker in `cycle_label`. The resolver was inert on every real ledger, green in unit tests and red live. **cycle-1194** then split the autoseal role out of `operator`. Both are in ADR-0081.
- **cycle-1433**: `Anchor` bound the first sibling of an ambiguous seq and exited 0. That silently moved the anchor backward over lines the operator believed were sealed.
- **2026-08-11** (console plane): `inboxmover` raw-appended unchained lines on almost every cycle, about 180 dense breaks, and `Rebaseline` chained from the tip, so it failed on exactly the foreign-tail damage it was built for. The fixes were `AppendLifecycle` and `appendChainedFromTail`. The lesson: repair commands must bind the file as it physically exists, not as sidecar state remembers it.
- **cycle-1677**: `OK: chain intact` was one string for two different claims, full-strict and anchored, and that let the line-1740 damage stay invisible. On the live ledger of about 141k lines, the sidecar recorded `anchor_seq=113890` while an in-band seal had moved the anchor to 136212. Reporting the sidecar's number would have misnamed the trusted prefix by about 22k lines, in the reassuring direction.
- **ADR-0101 S4a review (HIGH-1)**: the first observer was a Decorator whose promoted methods bypassed it; it was replaced by the `WithSignals` option.
- **Composition verdicts**: the reader and kernel verifier landed in cycle-786, the writer in cycle-787, and the RUNG 2 `method` field in cycle-941.
- **2026-10-06, ADR-0105 F5** ([incident](../../incidents/2026-10-06-carry-refused-ledger-verify-f5.md)): the identity carry wrote its diffs into the cycle worktree and its line into the project ledger. After the first worktree cleanup the plane ledger reported `BROKEN: line 154453 … composition-1766-audited.diff: no such file or directory`, and ship's whole-ledger Verify declined all six carries (1766 to 1810). The evidence store, the restore verb and the chained evidence record are the fix. The same review found that RUNG 0/2 wrote through `<worktree>/.evolve/ledger.jsonl`: a symlink to the plane ledger (core's `linkGuardDeps`) whose tip and lock were the worktree's own, so a rung-0/2 line would have forked the plane chain. `core` now passes the project ledger.
- **Throughput target**: the bash ledger cost roughly 10-20 ms per append (jq, tee, flock, sha256sum), and the Go target was at most 0.6× that. `BenchmarkAppendSerial` and `BenchmarkVerify` measure it.
- **Open: plain `Verify` cannot verify a sealed ledger.** Once a segment exists, the first live line chains from a sealed line, and `Verify` stops with "chained-genesis prev_hash mismatch" at line 0. Only `VerifyDeep` (`evolve ledger verify --deep`) passes. `evolve ledger anchor` runs plain `Verify` after recording, so anchoring on a sealed ledger records the anchor but exits 2. A comment on `Seal` once claimed that plain `Verify` keeps working after a seal; it was wrong.
- **Open: `TestVerify_DuplicatePrevHash` does not test duplicate detection.** Its injected line is adjacent and shares its parent, so the walk accepts it as a fork sibling, and only the stale tip fails the chain. The non-adjacent case is covered by `TestWalkChain_DuplicatePrevWithoutSiblingSignatureIsBroken`.
