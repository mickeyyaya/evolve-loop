# Recommendation — Netflix operating margin +1pp through the device experience

Goal: raise Netflix operating margin by 1 percentage point through the device experience. At
FY2025 revenue that is ≈$452M of operating income; FY2025 margin was 29.5% (E1). All numbers cite
`assumptions-and-evidence.md`. The three options each move a different mechanism: cost-to-serve
(Option 1), device-partner economics (Option 2) and churn through playback quality (Option 3).

**Timing basis.** All three options start in month 0. Each option's lag and ramp is a labelled
assumption, shown in the first row of the matrix. A "year N" effect is the annualized run-rate at
the end of month 12·N, at FY2025 revenue (E1).

## Recommendation

**Recommended: Option 3 — fix playback on the low-end TV long tail. Run Option 1 alongside it as a
no-regret complement.** The choice is made on the year-3 run-rate, the horizon on which the +1pp
goal can first come close.

Why the matrix favors it:

1. **Largest effect from year 2 onward.** On the shared timing basis, Option 3 gives
   +0.33pp at year 2 and +0.50pp at year 3. Option 2 gives +0.24pp and +0.34pp. Option 3 is also
   the only option still compounding at year 4 (A12). It does **not** lead in year 1: its 6-month
   build (A13) leaves +0.12pp, below Option 2's +0.15pp.
2. **Best on every non-magnitude row.** It is highly reversible, has no member-facing risk, and
   improves the device experience directly. Option 2 is hard to reverse (multi-year contracts), and
   its main downside, lost placement, damages the device surface the goal targets.
3. **Option 1 is complementary, not competing.** Option 1's AV1 rollout also cuts rebuffers 45%
   (E5), which is part of Option 3's fix set. It is certain in direction and has no member risk.
   Alone it cannot move the target (+0.10pp at full ramp).

Option 3 is **not** the fastest option to prove. Options 1 and 2 can each be sized in about a
quarter. Option 3's first read comes around month 9 (A13 build plus the A9 holdout). That is the
main cost of this choice, and the flip section says what the read-out would have to show to
reverse it.

**Honest target statement.** No single option substantiates +1pp. On the shared timing basis,
Options 3 + 1 give ≈ 0.17pp in year 1 and ≈ 0.60pp by year 3. Adding Option 2 **in parallel from month 0** gives
≈ 0.32pp in year 1, ≈ 0.94pp by year 3 (0.50 + 0.10 + 0.34) and ≈ 1.12pp by year 4
(0.63 + 0.10 + 0.39). So on base-case assumptions, **+1pp is not reached within three years by any
combination we can substantiate**. It is reached during year 4, and only if all three options start
in month 0. Option 2 cannot wait for a later wave: each quarter of delay forfeits another 12.5% of
its contract value until month 36, costing ≈0.05pp of its year-3 effect (A14). If the goal means
+1pp within 12 or 36 months, no option here supports it.

**Runner-up: Option 2 — device-partner economics.** It has the largest year-1 effect, and it is
the quickest to size. A5 is a ledger read and A6 a 90-day one-partner holdout, so both answer in
about one quarter. It ranks second for three reasons:

- Its year-3 effect is smaller (+0.34pp against +0.50pp).
- Its contracts are multi-year and hard to reverse.
- Its main risk is partner retaliation through worse placement. That is a partner reaction that
  neither probe measures. It first shows at the repriced renewals from month 4 (A14), and it
  damages the device surface the goal targets.

## Evidence that would flip the choice

The year-3 crossover follows from the matrix: Option 2 overtakes Option 3 once Option 3 shrinks
below 0.34 ÷ 0.50 = 0.68 of its base case. The thresholds below sit at that crossover. There is
no band between the magnitude crossover and the flip.

- **The cohort holdout shows a smaller retention lift.** Option 3 scales with the churn cut. If
  fixing the low-end device cohort reduces its monthly gross churn by **less than ≈0.10pp**
  (0.15pp × 0.68; A9 assumes 0.15pp), Option 2 has the larger year-3 effect, and the
  recommendation flips to Option 2.
- **The device cohort is smaller.** Option 3 also scales with the cohort's share of members. If
  telemetry shows **fewer than ≈17% of members** (25% × 0.68; A8 assumes 25%) have a low-end
  primary device, the recommendation flips to Option 2.
- **The build is slower.** If the delivery plan (A13 probe) puts the fix set later than ≈17 months,
  Option 3's year-3 effect falls below Option 2's, and the recommendation flips.

These would also flip it to Option 2:

- **The partner pool is larger than assumed.** If the internal ledger shows partner payments above
  **≈$1.03B/yr** (versus A5's $0.7B), Option 2's year-3 effect exceeds Option 3's.
- **The horizon is one year.** If the operator must show the result within 12 months, Option 2
  leads on the matrix's year-1 row. It should then lead the program, with Option 3 following.

This would demote Option 1 from complement to deprioritized:

- **Delivery cost is below $0.5B** (versus A1's $0.9B). The saving then falls under 0.06pp at full
  ramp and is not worth the certification push on margin grounds alone.
